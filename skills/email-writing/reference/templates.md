# Email templates

Shapes, not scripts. Fill with the user's voice from `MEMORY.md`. Every one
maps to the four-part body in `SKILL.md`: anchor → ask → enable → next step.

---

## 1. The ask (RACE)

```
Subject: <Thing needed> — <by when>

Hi <Name>,

<Anchor: one clause of shared context.>

Could you <specific action>? <Deadline or why-now.>

<Enable: the 1–3 facts, link, or options they need.>

<Next step: what happens after they act.>

<Sign-off>
```
Rule: if they must open an attachment to understand the ask, the ask is wrong.

---

## 2. Follow-up (no reply yet)

First follow-up — 3 working days. Second — 7 days. Then stop.

```
Subject: Re: <original subject>   ← same thread, never "Following up"

Hi <Name>,

Bringing this back up in case it slipped — <one-line restatement of the ask>.

<New information, a shortened option, or a deadline that changed.>

If it's not a priority right now, tell me and I'll park it.

<Sign-off>
```
Rules: never guilt ("I haven't heard back"), always add something new, always
offer an easy out.

---

## 3. Decline / saying no

```
Subject: <Topic> — can't take this on

Hi <Name>,

Thanks for thinking of me. I can't <do X> — <one honest reason, no essay>.

<Optional: the one alternative you can offer, or a person who can.>

<Sign-off>
```
Rules: no is in sentence two. No apology loop. No fake future ("maybe next
quarter") unless true. Short = respectful, not cold.

---

## 4. Apology / mistake

```
Subject: <What broke> — what happened and the fix

Hi <Name>,

<Plain statement of what went wrong. No passive voice — "I missed", not
"the deadline was missed".>

Here's the fix: <action, owner, date>.

To stop it recurring: <one concrete change>.

<Sign-off>
```
Rules: own it in sentence one. No "if anyone was affected". No blaming a tool,
a calendar, or a teammate. Fix outweighs apology 3:1 in word count.

---

## 5. Status update (STAR)

```
Subject: <Project> — week of <date>

Hi <Name>,

Headline: <on track / at risk / blocked — one word plus one clause.>

Done this week
- <result, not activity>

Next
- <item — owner — date>

Needs you
- <the single decision or unblock you want. If none, write "Nothing.">

<Sign-off>
```
Rules: "needs you" is why the email exists. Report outcomes, not effort.
Red/amber status appears in line one, never buried at the bottom.

---

## 6. Cold outreach

```
Subject: <Their world> + <your specific relevance>

Hi <Name>,

<Proof you know who they are — one specific, verifiable detail. Not flattery.>

<What you want, in one sentence.>

<Why it's worth their 15 minutes — one concrete result or asset.>

Worth a short call? If not, no hard feelings.

<Sign-off>
```
Rules: under 100 words. One link maximum. Never "I'll keep this brief" (just
be brief). Never attach anything on first contact.

---

## 7. Intro (connecting two people)

```
Subject: Intro: <A> <> <B>

<A>, meet <B> — <one line on B and why A cares>.
<B>, meet <A> — <one line on A and why B cares>.

<The reason this intro exists, one sentence.>

Handing over to you two. Moving myself to bcc.

<Sign-off>
```
Rule: always double-opt-in first. Never intro without both sides' yes.

---

## 8. Escalation

```
Subject: <Issue> — need a decision by <date>

Hi <Name>,

<Issue in one sentence, with its business impact.>

Tried so far: <one line.>
Blocked by: <one line — a thing, not a person, unless unavoidable.>

Asking you to: <the specific decision or authority needed.>
If I don't hear back by <date>, I'll <your default action>.

<Sign-off>
```
Rules: name the impact, not the frustration. Always include your default
action — it converts a complaint into a decision.

---

## 9. Negotiation / pushback

```
Subject: <Topic> — a counter

Hi <Name>,

Thanks — I want to make this work. Two things don't yet:

1. <Item> — <why, and what would fix it.>
2. <Item> — <why, and what would fix it.>

Everything else I'm good with. If <specific change>, I can confirm by <date>.

<Sign-off>
```
Rules: agree loudly on what works before you disagree. Every objection ships
with the fix that resolves it. Never negotiate a number you were told to flag.

---

## 10. Scheduling

```
Subject: <Purpose> — 30 min this week?

Hi <Name>,

<Purpose in one line — what we'll decide, not "catch up".>

Any of these work (GST):
- Tue 14:00
- Wed 10:00
- Thu 16:00

Or send an invite whenever suits.

<Sign-off>
```
Rule: three concrete slots with a timezone beats "let me know your
availability." Always state the meeting's decision.

---

## 11. Thank-you / after a meeting

```
Subject: Thanks — <topic> next steps

Hi <Name>,

Good conversation today, particularly on <specific point>.

What I took away:
- <decision>
- <action — owner — date>

Shout if I mis-captured anything.

<Sign-off>
```
Rule: this email's real job is locking down the actions. Gratitude is the
wrapper, the action list is the payload.

---

## 12. Bad-news delivery

```
Subject: <Thing> — <plain statement of the change>

Hi <Name>,

<The news, in sentence one. No runway.>

Why: <one or two sentences, honest, no hedging.>
What it means for you: <concrete impact.>
What happens now: <action, owner, date.>

I'm available today if you want to talk it through.

<Sign-off>
```
Rules: no sandwich. No "unfortunately" as a shield. Offer a live conversation.
